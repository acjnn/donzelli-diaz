## Copy and tone

This applies to all user-facing text: headings, nav, buttons, labels, blurbs, footers, alt text, page titles, and meta descriptions.

- Write like a normal person describing their own work, in first person ("I", "my"). Lorenzo is an engineer, not an editor, author, or publisher.
- Use standard website terms: Blog, Posts, About, Contact, CV. Not "journal", "dispatches", "correspondence", "doors", "ledger", "dossier", "folio", "the author", "from the editor", "fresh off the press", or anything in that family.
- No newspaper, print-shop, letter-writing, or military role-play. No fake issue or volume numbers ("Folio 001", "Vol. VI").
- No marketing voice: no taglines, slogans, buzzwords, or clever subtitles. If a label or subtitle doesn't tell the reader anything, delete it rather than making it quirky.
- Don't describe the design or typography in the copy ("Set in ...", "Hand-inked in the browser").
- Before adding text, ask whether you'd say it out loud to a colleague. If not, rewrite it plainly.

The site's editorial visual style (serif type, ink frames, section numerals) is not a reason to write in an editorial voice.
